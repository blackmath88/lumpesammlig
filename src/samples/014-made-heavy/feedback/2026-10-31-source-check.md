## Observation

**Ivaschenko, N. A., "Brutalist Architecture in Russian and Foreign Art History after Reyner Banham", *Articult* 4(48), 2022, pp. 6–16** (report 02 [^1]; report 01 [^2]). A historiographic survey of the literature, not a study of buildings.
- Confirms that Vassiliev (2016 conference paper) explicitly asserts the existence of Soviet brutalism and dates its emergence to the 1970s.
- Quotes Vassiliev's device list, including "мощные, часто расширяющиеся кверху, визуально усиленные пилоны" (powerful, often upward-widening, visually reinforced pylons) and the division of glazing "при помощи импостов и рёбер солнцезащиты" (with mullions and sun-screen ribs). Report 02 §4.2 and §6.1 are supported.
- Lists the Moscow Palace of Youth with Belopolsky and 1982–1988 as a name in Vassiliev's examples. No analysis of the building. Report 02 §6.1 states no more than this.
- Quotes Vassiliev on "отсутствие какой-либо считываемой снаружи структуры" (the absence of any structure readable from outside) as a general characteristic. Report 02 §4.3 attributes this correctly. The further step in §4.3, that the image of support and the actual engineering are not always identical, is **not** in Ivaschenko's text as fetched; the article cites Rudolph and Lelle on brutalist buildings working "as image" and the term "structural expressionism", which is adjacent but not the same claim.
- Not on the page: load paths, engineering, photographs or plans; Vassiliev's own paper, cited by title only.

**Heathcote, E., "Soviet brutalist architecture: beyond the genre's striking image", *Wallpaper\**, 13 November 2024** (report 02 [^2]; report 01 [^1]).
- Confirms the Robotics and Cybernetics Centre: Artyushin and Savin, completed 1986, "a true cathedral of science". **Does not name Saint Petersburg**; report 01 §3 adds the city.
- Confirms the Druzhba Sanatorium: Vasilevsky, 1983, Yalta, UFO-like ("so odd that the Pentagon mistook it for a new rocket launch pad"). "Gear-like", used in report 02 §6.3 and in this study's "ring on legs" preset label, is not on the page.
- Confirms the criticism of the photogenic image: "fetishised in publishing and across social media ... based solely on the image."
- Mentions the housing problem and singular ceremonial buildings as "socialist equivalents to the cathedrals of the past", but **not** the collective-versus-individual framing that report 01 makes its defining tension.
- Says Soviet architects were "well aware of the emergence of brutalism elsewhere in Europe"; **does not** mention Le Corbusier, British brutalism or Japanese architecture, which report 02 §2 attributes to [^1][^2].

## Interpretation

The study's behaviour depends on the general vocabulary and on the caution about readable structure. Both survive: the vocabulary is Vassiliev's as quoted by Ivaschenko, and the unreadable-structure remark is his. What weakens is the sharper formulation the study adopted from the report, "the image of support and the engineering need not agree", which is the report's inference. Experiment 01's "rhetorical pier" is therefore grounded in a reasonable reading of Vassiliev, not in his words. The page prose and `sample.md` say "after Vassiliev's observation that the image of support and the engineering need not agree"; "after" is doing more work than it should.

The collective-versus-individual tension that reading 02 preserves is report 01's own synthesis, not Heathcote's, so far as the fetched text shows. Reading 02 should be read as resting on the report, not on the cited journalism.

Report 01's "Saint Petersburg" is correct as a matter of geography but is not supplied by the source it cites.
