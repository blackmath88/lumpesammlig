# 002 — Paper and ink, nothing concrete-coloured

Status: accepted, implemented 2026-10-31. Scope: **this study only**. Retrospective reconstruction from `../sample.md` ("The surfaces of this page are paper and ink, not concrete, on purpose: the encounter gave us a language of bearing, suspension, repetition and trace, and no permission to paint anything grey") and the page's evidence note.

**Choice.** No grey, no concrete texture, no photographic reference to buildings. The page surfaces are paper and ink.

**Alternatives the record names.** Concrete-coloured or textured surfaces. Implicitly, any visual citation of the source.

**Reasons.** The encounter contains no photograph taken by the author and no visit; there is no visual evidence to be faithful to. The reading is behavioural, and a concrete palette would invite the review failure the repository names first: decorative translation. Report 02 §3 also warns that a building's visible surface is often not its structure, and §7 that the photogenic image displaces meaning.

**Trade-offs.** The page gives up atmosphere. A visitor who expects brutalism to look like brutalism meets a white page with text on piers, and must read to find out why. `sample.md` concedes one exception: the formwork grain in experiment 04 is decorative by the repository's own test and kept only because that experiment is about trace as surface.

**Grounding.** `docs/CONCEPT.md` §4 (transformation is semantic, not stylistic); `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §12 design test.

**Revisit if.** The study acquires visual evidence of its own, or if a separate material study of the archaic-futuristic surface (`../readings/03-archaic-and-futuristic.md`) is started. Even then, this page's decision stands; the material study would be its own entry.

**Not a default.** Other studies must not inherit paper and ink from this one. The study index says so.
