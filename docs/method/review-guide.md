# Review guide

Questions for judging a proposition or an implemented experiment. They apply to behavioural transformations; a study that declares itself a material or perceptual study is reviewed with the questions at the end instead. The guide is for finding weaknesses honestly, not for rejecting unresolved encounters or beautiful failures, which remain legitimate collection entries.

## The three questions

Before calling a behavioural transformation complete:

1. If the colours, textures and references to the source disappeared, would the behaviour, structure or interaction still carry something specific from the encounter?
2. Could ten unrelated sources produce this exact experiment by swapping labels and colours?
3. What in the encounter gives us permission to make this move?

If the first answer is no or the second is yes, revisit the transformation or describe the result honestly as a material or visual study. Do not attach an abstract story after the fact.

## Failure patterns to look for

**Generic analogy.** The reading stops at a metaphor ("layers", "connection", "weight") that any source could supply, and the interface is the metaphor's stock illustration. Test: name the nearest peer in interface design and say what this study does that the peer does not.

**Decorative translation.** Appearance is reproduced; nothing about what the visitor can do has changed. Palette, texture, grain or silhouette do all the work. Legitimate only if the study says it is a material study.

**Excessive abstraction.** The proposition is defensible in prose but has no first screen, components, action or visible consequence. Ask to see the state change.

**Research-tool drift.** The source has become a dashboard, filter panel or note-taking tool because those are easy to build. Ask whether the encounter asked for retrieval at all.

**Composite instead of selection.** Several readings are blended into one page so that none is tested. Prefer separate experiments or a chosen one.

**Poor usability.** The signature interaction is unreachable by keyboard, breaks at phone width, depends on motion with no reduced-motion path, has unclear states, or fails without JavaScript where static content could have been served. Specificity does not excuse this.

**Unsupported claims.** Provenance, history, physical behaviour, historical dates, user feedback or verification results that are not in the evidence or were not actually run. Check the record for the words "verified", "tested", "users", "originally" and ask for the basis of each.

**Default creep.** A previous study's palette, layout, form or rule is reused without a reason from this encounter. The [study index](study-index.md) names, per study, what must not become a default.

**Overwritten evidence.** Later interpretation has replaced or edited source material instead of sitting beside it.

## Reviewing the record, not only the page

A reader who has not seen the conversation should be able to trace each experiment to evidence and a reading, distinguish observation from inference from invention, find the readings that were rejected and why, and see what is unresolved. If the record claims a conversation, decision or test happened, it should say where that is preserved or state that it is a retrospective reconstruction.

## For material and perceptual studies

- Which observable qualities produce the intended material impression, and are they named?
- Does the impression survive at actual interface size, not only enlarged?
- Can it coexist with legibility, clear states and responsive interaction?
- Does the note say plainly that this is a material study rather than a behavioural transformation?

## Writing a review

Separate observation (what you saw or ran) from interpretation (what you think it means) from proposal (what might change). Identify the version reviewed by commit. Record the outcome once known. The shape of a feedback note is in [records.md](records.md).
