# 001 — Check the load-bearing link of a source chain

Status: proposed
Scope: studies whose grounding passes through secondary research (a report, a summary, an assistant's synthesis) rather than through first-hand evidence or a primary source read directly
Supporting examples: `src/samples/014-made-heavy/feedback/2026-10-31-source-check.md`; `src/samples/014-made-heavy/research/sources.md` (Check results); `src/samples/014-made-heavy/readings/01-tectonic-language.md` (What would weaken it)
Counterexamples: studies 012 and 013, where `docs/research/012-013-held-place-live-circuits.md` records a bounded set of primary sources consulted before implementation, so no chain needed checking; studies 001 to 011, grounded in the author's own object or memory, where there is no chain at all
Approval: none yet

## Observation

Study 014 took "the image of support and the actual engineering need not agree" from a research report that attributed it to Vassiliev via Ivaschenko. When the Ivaschenko article was fetched, Vassiliev's quoted remark was narrower (buildings with no structure readable from outside); the sharper formulation was the report's inference. The study's page prose says "after Vassiliev's observation". The experiment itself is unaffected, but its stated permission is one step weaker than written. The chain was report → review article → conference paper, and only the first link had been read when the study was published.

## Proposed change

Add to [study-practice.md](../study-practice.md) §1, after the four kinds of statement: *When a study's permission for a move rests on a secondary source, name the chain (who says that who says it) and read at least the link that carries the claim before the study is published. If that link cannot be read, say so in the research note and phrase the grounding as "as reported by", not "after".*

One sentence, not a new step. It applies only where a chain exists.

## What would show it was a mistake

If, over several researched studies, reading the load-bearing link never changed a formulation, the sentence is paperwork and should be withdrawn. If it regularly delays studies whose experiments do not depend on the attribution at all (as 014's did not), the scope should narrow to claims the page prose attributes by name.
